## Early Access Pricing

Here's the reframe I'd suggest: **you don't need to choose between "commercial product" and "support this project."** There's a third framing that's both honest and strategically sound:

> **Early access pricing.**

This acknowledges that the product is in active development without apologizing for it, signals that pricing will increase as the product matures (creating urgency), and positions paying users as partners in building something — not customers of a finished product, and not donors to a hobby.

Practically, it looks like this:

**On the pricing page:**

> *Drive.io is in active development. Early access pricing locks in your rate permanently — pricing increases as the product matures. You're not just getting access, you're helping shape what gets built next.*

**On the free tier:**

> *Free forever for individual developers — no credit card required. We'd rather you use it and tell us what's broken than not use it at all.*

**For the Developer tier ($19/mo):**

> *Early access rate. Includes direct access to the founding team — bug reports, feature requests, and integration questions get a real response.*

---

## Bottom Line

Don't position it as support. Position it as **early access to infrastructure you're already using anyway** — with the honest caveat that it's actively being built, and the genuine upside that early adopters get locked-in pricing and a direct line to the founders.

That framing is honest, it's commercially credible, and it actually makes a stronger YC narrative: *"We launched at early access pricing, got 10 paying developers in 8 weeks, and every one of them is actively using the API."*
